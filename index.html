Number Theory

Olympiad Strategy Blueprint

Essential cognitive shortcuts used by national Math Olympiad champions to factor large composites in seconds.

Strategy 01

Parity & Modulo 5 Isolation

Always strip all factors of 2 and 5 immediately by inspecting the last digit. Repeated division reduces composite magnitude exponentially.

Strategy 02

Sum of Digits & Modulo 9

Compute the digital sum: if the sum of digits is a multiple of 9, the number is divisible by 3² = 9. If divisible by 3, extract 3 and evaluate the quotient immediately.

Strategy 03

Difference of Squares: a² - b²

For odd composites, check if N + b² = a². For example, 899 = 900 - 1 = 30² - 1² = 29 × 31. Many Olympiad problems disguise factors via nearby perfect squares.

Strategy 04

Alternating Digit Sum (Mod 11)

Calculate alternating sum of digits: d₀ - d₁ + d₂ - d₃.... If the result is a multiple of 11 (including 0), 11 divides the integer cleanly without remainder.

Strategy 05

Truncation & Factor 7 / 13

For 7: Double the last digit and subtract from the rest (Rest - 2×Last). For 13: Multiply last digit by 4 and add to the rest (Rest + 4×Last).

Strategy 06

Pollard's Polynomial Intuition

When human mental bounds are exceeded, computer engines use f(x) = (x² + 1) mod n. The birthday paradox guarantees cycle collisions in O(p^0.5) steps.
